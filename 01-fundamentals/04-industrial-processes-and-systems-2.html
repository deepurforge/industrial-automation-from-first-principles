04
The foundation

The universal structure of a process

Almost every industrial process, regardless of industry, reduces to this shape.

Animation — disturbances and control action both acting on the process
  • Inputs — material, energy, or information entering the process.
  • Outputs — product, waste, or information leaving it.
  • Disturbances — unplanned changes acting on the process from outside.
  • Control actions — deliberate adjustments keeping the process on target.
05
What enters

Inputs — material, energy, information

Inputs are not only electrical signals. A setpoint entered by an operator is just as much an input as the raw water flowing into a tank.

CategoryExamples
MaterialWater, raw material, fuel, chemicals, parts
EnergyElectrical, mechanical, thermal, compressed air, hydraulic
InformationProduction command, setpoint, recipe, operator command
06
What leaves

Outputs — physical or informational

Product, waste, heat, motion, pressure, flow, electrical energy, information.

A process that makes a perfect product but reports nothing about itself is invisible to everyone trying to manage it. Informational outputs matter as much as physical ones.

A window into the process

Measurement

13
The simplest — and most fragile

Open-loop control

Command → Controller → Actuator → Process. Notice what's missing: no arrow leads back. No feedback.

Mini trend — commanded vs. actual, no correction
A heater switched ON for 10 minutes, regardless of actual temperature — works, until conditions change from whatever they were when the timing was calculated.
14
One arrow changes everything

Closed-loop control

Setpoint → Controller → Actuator → Process → Sensor → back to Controller. That single arrow is the entire concept of feedback.

Animation — the loop that corrects itself, forever
This loop never stops running. It is, without exaggeration, the single most important structural idea in all of industrial automation.

System thinking

Systems are made of subsystems

A pressure sensor drifting out of calibration doesn't just show a wrong number — it can move an actuator incorrectly, changing the physical process.

Animation — a fault propagating through subsystems
18
A choice, not a fact

System boundaries

Where does a system begin and end? The same pump can be inside one boundary and outside another.

Troubleshooting, documentation, and responsibility all depend on everyone agreeing where one system's boundary ends and the next begins.
